Fifteen first-person-neutral STAR stories exported from the Notion STAR Story Bank database, kept in the owner's own wording as a source for interview answers and for writing style. Source file: `_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md`.

Rules for reading this file:
- Tier is `single-source` for every story (one Notion database). No story is `verified` unless the user says so.
- Numbers match the claims already in `kb/roles/*` unless a note says otherwise. Session counts follow the user ruling in `kb/_user_decisions.md` (18+ sessions, 6,000+ attendees; scan scope 50 states + DC).
- Omitted on purpose: the Deploy For, Primary Skill and Secondary Skill relations, because they point at job-application and Notes content that the user excluded from the import.
- No employment type (full-time/part-time) is recorded for any role.

### S001
title: Zero-Slip Delivery Across Six Grant-Funded Workstreams
role: ncmw-project-coordinator
tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]
situation: A newly formed Substance Use Continuum team at NCMW, a 3,200+ member behavioral health association, needed one person running day-to-day operations across 6+ concurrent grant-funded workstreams, with federal funder deadlines that shifted regularly.
task: Keep every workstream aligned to federal funder, partner, and member requirements without anything falling through as timelines moved.
action: Built centralized deliverable tracking systems (Asana, Excel, Airtable) integrated across HubSpot CRM, grant portal, Google Drive/OneDrive/SharePoint. Standardized workflows and documentation templates across 4 internal departments via structured Slack channels and recurring leadership status reports. Triaged competing staff requests and workstream priorities independently, escalating scope, resource, or risk issues to leadership rather than letting them stall.
result: 100% on-time deliverable completion despite evolving federal funder timelines; cut handoff friction and deliverable turnaround for a newly formed program office.
earned_secret: When timelines are externally set by federal funders and can shift without warning, the tracking system itself has to be the stable thing, not any one person's memory. Build the system before you build the habit.

### S002
title: Sole Owner of a 12-Module Course, Start to Finish
role: ncmw-project-coordinator
tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]
situation: NCMW needed a foundational e-learning course on SUD, overdose response, and recovery support, tied to federal HHS/SAMHSA grant compliance requirements, with no dedicated instructional design staff. One module specifically needed to teach 11 clinical diagnostic criteria (DSM-5) to a lay audience that included impacted family members, not just professionals.
task: Serve as sole instructional designer, content author, and project manager for a 12-module course, from architecture through LMS deployment, while making clinical diagnostic language usable and non-stigmatizing for someone with no clinical background.
action: Authored all 12 modules in Articulate Rise 360 (DSM-5 criteria, brain physiology, stages of change, treatment pathways, overdose response), including an animated naloxone reversal instructional video. Rather than presenting the diagnostic criteria as a static list, built an interactive criteria explorer that let learners click through and engage with each criterion directly, paired with plain-language framing throughout. Produced AI avatar narration (Synthesia) and brand-compliant visuals (Canva) meeting Section 508/WCAG 2.1 and SAMHSA plain-language standards. Administered the course on LearnWorlds LMS and synthesized learner feedback (Jotform/SurveyMonkey) into improvement recommendations for leadership.
result: Full course built end to end by one person, turning clinical diagnostic criteria into an accessible, non-stigmatizing learning tool inside a fully compliant product, positioned as a broad-access resource for family members, people in recovery, and cross-sector professionals.
earned_secret: A course aimed at people who might be in crisis or grieving has to be built differently than professional training. Accessibility and plain-language standards are the difference between a resource someone in a hard moment can use and one they'll bounce off of.
related_kb_claims: A-ncmw-project-coordinator-57, -58, -64

### S003
title: Turning a Compliance Exercise Into a Deliverable Directors Adopted
role: ncmw-project-coordinator
tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]
situation: NCMW needed to select edtech/AI vendor platforms (HeyGen, Articulate, LearnWorlds, Synthesia, Mindsmith) for course development, each carrying a different exposure profile across grants, privacy, accessibility, AI governance, and security regulation (roughly 26 to 28 requirements per vendor).
task: Evaluate vendor platforms against the full regulatory matrix and produce something usable, not just an audit.
action: Built the compliance mapping in parallel with content and platform decisions rather than as an end-of-process audit, validating specific build choices, like the AI avatar narration approach, against federal AI and privacy guidance before committing rather than after. Constructed a structured, repeatable compliance matrix (Meets / Pass / Grantee Control / N/A) mapping each vendor against 2 CFR Part 200, FERPA, COPPA, Section 508/WCAG, OMB M-25-21/22, NIST AI RMF, SOC 2, FedRAMP, ISO 27001, and GDPR/CCPA. result: Produced audit-ready compliance checklists and procurement recommendation memos that senior directors adopted as a formal grant deliverable, and the AI-narrated approach shipped without a rebuild since it had been validated up front.
earned_secret: Regulatory research only creates value once it's translated into a decision a non-specialist leader can act on. The matrix format mattered as much as the research, turning 28 rows of citations into something a director could scan in five minutes and sign off on. Building the compliance check in parallel with the build decisions, instead of after them, is what turned it from paperwork into something that actually prevented a rebuild.

### S005
title: A 50-State Legal Landscape Scan Built to Survive Contact With Reality
role: ncmw-project-coordinator
tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]
situation: Leadership needed to understand how SUD-related involuntary commitment law varied across all 50 states plus DC, information that was fragmented and not centralized anywhere.
task: Build a comparative resource that made that fragmentation usable for strategic decision-making.
action: Constructed a structured matrix across 51 jurisdictions with 18 data fields each (statute citations, legal standards, max commitment days, enforcement notes), applying 7 strictness categories to rank-order jurisdictions. Cross-referenced legal text against real enforcement data rather than relying on the statutes alone, and explicitly flagged where no centralized dataset existed and where information came from defender manuals and training materials rather than hard utilization data.
result: Produced a synthesized comparative resource, for example Minnesota's 365-day maximum versus Alaska's 30-day standard, and Kentucky's unusually dual-track legal standard, giving leadership a usable strategic view of an area with no centralized authority, while flagging unreliable underlying data instead of reporting false confidence.
earned_secret: The most valuable part of a 50-state scan isn't the data you find, it's being honest about where the data doesn't exist. Flagging the gaps was as important to the deliverable's credibility as the rows that were fully populated.

### S007
title: Building a Parallel-Processing System During a Housing Voucher Shortage
role: sanctuary-centers-housing-coordinator
tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]
situation: Managing a caseload of 20-30 adults with serious mental illness, co-occurring substance use, and complex trauma, including individuals navigating reentry from incarceration, and coordinating housing placement across five concurrent federal/state/local subsidy programs (Section 8 HCV, Project-Based Vouchers, HUD-VASH, Emergency Housing Vouchers), each with distinct documentation and eligibility requirements. A post-pandemic voucher shortage hit and demand outpaced supply.
task: Keep every client's housing paperwork moving despite five different program bureaucracies and a sudden supply crunch, without any case stalling.

### S011
title: Sitting Beside the System's Blind Spot
role: sanctuary-centers-housing-coordinator
tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]
situation: Coordinating care for a caseload of 20-30 adults experiencing homelessness, many with co-occurring substance use and complex trauma, in the post-pandemic landscape when services were scarce and demand outpaced supply. Documentation portals and voucher applications assumed a baseline of technology literacy that not everyone had; the barrier wasn't just device ownership, it was a knowledge gap built into the interface itself.
task: Help people actually complete applications and access resources, not just refer them to a system they'd bounce off of.
action: Sat beside clients and completed forms with them directly, translating jargon-heavy instructions into plain language in real time, in effect adapting the process to the person instead of asking the person to adapt to the process, rather than pointing them at a self-serve portal that wasn't built for them.
result: A concrete, formative realization that reshaped how she approaches every system since: a solution only works if it's designed for the person using it, not the policy it's meant to satisfy.
earned_secret: A lack of evidence rarely explains why care doesn't reach someone. The real gap is usually a structural lack of cognizance, systems that were never built to notice how needs and barriers shift from one community, or one person, to the next.

tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]

### S013
title: The Memo That Argued Against Itself
role: ncmw-project-coordinator
tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]
situation: Leadership needed a strategic proposal defining two real paths forward for a behavioral healthtech consulting offering: a full-scale option with a first-year loss and stronger second-year upside, versus a bootstrapped option with immediate margin but slower long-term scale.
task: Give leadership a genuinely useful decision document rather than a case built to advocate for one preferred outcome.
action: Built out both options with equal rigor, full staffing plans, tiered pricing, and multi-year financial projections for each (full-scale model projecting Year 2 revenue of $2.875M, bootstrapped model projecting Year 1 revenue of $500K at roughly 40% margin), deliberately constructing the strongest honest case for the option not being personally recommended so the memo would read as objective rather than as advocacy.
result: Delivered a complete, dual-option decision document with full financial modeling for both paths, giving leadership a real, quantified choice instead of a pre-decided recommendation dressed up as analysis.
earned_secret: The easiest way to lose a leader's trust in a recommendation is to let the alternative look weak on purpose. Building the option you don't prefer with the same rigor as the one you do is what makes the eventual recommendation credible, not the recommendation itself.

### S014
title: Mapping Money Before Medicine
role: ncmw-project-coordinator
tier: single-source
sources: [_raw/notion/star-story-bank.md]
situation: Leadership needed an environmental scan of ibogaine-assisted therapy, a Schedule I compound with real clinical promise but a politically fraught, uncertain path to legitimacy.
task: Give leadership something more predictive than a literature summary, an honest read on what would actually become adoptable in practice, not just what looked promising on paper.
action: Reviewed 12 peer-reviewed studies and an active clinical trial (NCT05029401), and, treating it as a separate analytical track from the clinical data, mapped the funding flow and political capital behind the intervention, including a $1.7M DemeRx grant and a $14M NIDA/Gilgamesh grant, profiling the federal agencies and researchers driving it forward.
result: The scan identified the leading near-term candidates and separated clinical, safety, and funding/political factors into independent tracks, so leadership could weigh them on their own terms rather than getting one blended, falsely confident recommendation.
earned_secret: Clinical promise and real-world adoption run on different clocks. A treatment can be scientifically ready long before it's politically or financially viable, and leadership needed to see those two timelines separately, not folded into one number.
