### Her 2: founding-phase work

> One of my strongest examples is from the National Council, where I supported a relatively new program operations structure managing multiple federally funded workstreams and a growing number of external relationships.
>
> The challenge was that our work had expanded faster than some of the operational infrastructure supporting it. As we transitioned more work into Asana, there wasn't always a consistent process for making sure an external commitment became an assigned, trackable internal action.
>
> The first thing I focused on was creating a reliable source of information about our active commitments.
>
> I started by consolidating the essentials: what needed to happen, who owned it, what the actual external deadline was, what internal milestones were required, and whether we were waiting on another person or department.
>
> From there, I helped build more structured workflows connecting Excel and Airtable tracking with Asana, HubSpot, and our grant portal.
>
> Because we were transitioning systems, I didn't assume that assigning a task in Asana meant everyone had necessarily received or acted on it. I manually verified important assignments and used Outlook invitations, email notifications, and follow-ups to make sure time-sensitive commitments reached the right people.
>
> I also developed a practice of recording changes as soon as they occurred, whether that involved a revised deadline, a new partner instruction, or a shift in ownership, and then reconciling those updates with the relevant project system.
>
> Over time, that became part of a more consistent leadership reporting process covering progress, upcoming deadlines, and operational risks.
>
> The result was that leadership had much better visibility across six or more concurrent workstreams, and we maintained 100% on-time deliverable completion even as external timelines changed.
>
> What I took from that experience is that building infrastructure in a founding-phase environment doesn't necessarily mean introducing a complicated new system. It means first making sure important information is captured accurately, responsibilities are clear, and the team has a dependable process for acting on it.

Process, as pasted (steps 1 to 6):

> 1. Intake: New request or commitment arrives through Outlook, a meeting, partner communication or grant portal.
>
> 2. Capture: Enter or update the central tracker immediately, preserving the source details and most current instructions.
>
> 3. Assignment: Convert the commitment into a task, assign ownership and establish internal milestones in Asana.
>
> 4. Notification: Use Outlook invitations or reminders for date-sensitive actions, checking that recipients receive the correct information.
>
> 5. Reconciliation: Verify that tracker updates, assignments and calendar details remain consistent as plans change.
>
> 6. Reporting: Surface upcoming deadlines, missing information, blockers and decisions requiring leadership attention.
