## What interests you most about working with academic health centers and global health leaders?

Good ideas in health professions education rarely travel on their own. Someone has to carry them from one institution to the next in a form the next institution can use. That connecting work is what interests me most. At the National Council for Mental Wellbeing I supported national programming for CCBHC and FQHC healthcare professionals, working with subject matter experts and federal and provider partners. Academic health centers and global health leaders are the people whose work I would be helping reach further. I have not worked inside an academic health center, so I would start by listening to how they already collaborate.

## AAHCI is a membership organization. How would you approach building and maintaining relationships with AAHCI member institutions and their representatives?

I would start by learning each institution: its priorities, its programs, and who the right contact is. Then I would make follow-through the habit. At NCMW, a 3,200+ member association, I was a primary point of contact for member organizations seeking technical assistance and resources, and I produced same-day meeting notes and tracked action items to completion. Members notice when a promise is kept. I would answer quickly, send the notes, close every action item, and tell members what their peers are doing so each contact brings something back to them.

## Share any experience you have supporting virtual programs, such as webinars, virtual roundtables, or online meetings.

Virtual programming is a core part of my current work. At NCMW I moderate national training webinars, office hours, and community of practice calls in Zoom for CCBHC and FQHC healthcare professionals, running setup, introductions, live polls, Q&A, and chat. I also coordinate registration, scheduling, learning materials, and learner communications from planning to closeout, tracking registrations and attendance in Airtable. I organized and facilitated virtual meetings in Microsoft Teams across four internal departments and 15+ external partner organizations, preparing agendas and following up on decisions and next steps.
