Dear Hiring Manager,

Access to expert care rarely fails because the expertise does not exist. It fails because the people who hold it and the people who need it have no dependable way to find each other, and no one is tending the connection once it is made. MAVEN Project, which builds and deploys a network of expert physician volunteers to support providers in safety net clinics and community health centers, is the clearest example I have seen of an organization that treats that connection as the work itself. That is why I am applying for the Program Coordinator role.

That understanding became concrete at Sanctuary Centers, where I provided Housing First case management for a concurrent caseload of 20 to 30 adults with serious mental illness, co-occurring substance use, and complex trauma histories. I served as the primary liaison among clients, landlords, clinical teams, and partner agencies, and as the link between our co-located Integrated Health Clinic and the housing programs, scheduling and tracking preventative-care appointments. Much of the job was keeping real-time HMIS documentation accurate enough to satisfy HUD, the county, and CenCal Health, and building a standardized document checklist so that applications moved in parallel instead of in a line. Systems built in isolation from each other cost real people real time.

That instinct carried into UC Santa Barbara, where I was the primary participant liaison for studies on sensitive family mental health topics, managing screening, scheduling, and follow-up, while supporting IRB protocol development and human subjects compliance documentation across concurrent studies. Keeping participants engaged on difficult subjects and keeping the paperwork audit-ready turned out to be one job seen from two sides. People stay in a program when the logistics around them are quiet and reliable.

That discipline scaled at the National Council for Mental Wellbeing, where I directed day-to-day operations across 6+ concurrent grant-funded workstreams. For national training programming, I coordinated registration, scheduling, and learner communications, tracked attendance in Airtable, moderated webinars and office hours in Zoom, administered the LearnWorlds LMS, and resolved day-of technical issues for presenters and learners. I managed continuing education (CE) compliance by reviewing attendance data and awarding training credits, then turned post-session feedback into recommendations that leadership adopted. I also handled logistics for 18+ conference sessions serving 6,000+ attendees at NatCon26, and assisted cross-functional performance improvement work for telemedicine and telehealth quality improvement operations. The lesson held: programs like this run on someone owning the schedule, the records, and the follow-through.

I want to be direct about one thing. My continuing education experience is attendance review and credit awarding, not ACCME accreditation itself, and I have not prepared ACCME reports or worked inside a CME accreditation cycle. What I bring instead is audit-ready documentation built across federal grant reporting, HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 recordkeeping, and IRB compliance, along with a habit of learning a standard well enough to document against it. I would rather learn MAVEN's accreditation requirements from the Director of Programs and the team already doing that work than assume my adjacent experience transfers without adjustment.

The path from direct service, to research, to national coordination has taught me the same thing three different ways. Expertise reaches the people who need it when someone does the quiet work of scheduling, documenting, and following through. I would welcome the opportunity to bring that work to MAVEN's volunteer physicians and the clinics they serve.

Sincerely,
Devika C. Dixit
