# Writing style guide (draft, awaiting user approval)

Use for cover letters and application narrative. For resume bullets, follow `kb/roles/*` canonical wording and bracketed alternatives instead. Facts always come from the KB, never from this file.

## Cover letter structure (repeats across letters)
1. Salutation, then an opening paragraph that starts with a general observation about how systems or services fail people, not with "I am applying". Example openers: "People's needs rarely separate as cleanly as the systems built to meet them" (aphsa); "Financial support systems rarely fail because the resources inside them are missing" (ndi); "Global health work rarely struggles for lack of proof that it works" (partners-in-health). The pattern "X rarely Y because Z" recurs in 3 of the 4 letters read in full.
2. The organization is named as "the clearest example I have seen" of that insight in practice, using a specific program or initiative of theirs (aphsa: ACCESS initiative; governors-pha: sharing data across state lines). The role title is named at the end of the first paragraph.
3. A three-step arc, one paragraph each, in this order: Sanctuary Centers (housing caseload, five subsidy programs, voucher shortage), UC Santa Barbara (research, trust with different communities), National Council (national scale). Transition lines: "That understanding became concrete at...", "That instinct carried into...", "That discipline scaled at...". Present in governors-pha, ndi, partners-in-health, aphsa (variants of the same transitions).
4. One candor paragraph that begins "I want to be direct about one thing" and names a real gap (for example no direct state-government experience in governors-pha; no disability benefits counseling in ndi; no global health organization in partners-in-health), then states what is brought instead and a learning-first posture ("I'd expect to spend time learning how X already works before assuming I know how to move faster"). Found in 7 of 10 letters.
5. Closing paragraph that restates the arc: "The path from [direct service] to [research] to [national coordination] has taught me the same thing three different ways" (governors-pha, ndi, partners-in-health, phi), then a short sentence of interest in the team. Sign-off "Sincerely, Devika C. Dixit".

## Recurring phrases and moves
- Refrain used as a thesis: "Systems built in isolation from each other cost real people real time" (aphsa, governors-pha, ndi, phi), once with "and sometimes a roof" (aphsa).
- Contrast construction "not X, but Y" or "It was not a paperwork fix. It was a recognition that..." (governors-pha, aphsa, partners-in-health).
- A lesson is drawn from each stage in a single plain closing sentence ("The lesson stuck: coordination determines whether services actually reach anyone", ndi).
- Credibility comes from one or two concrete numbers per stage (caseload of 20 to 30, five subsidy programs, six or more workstreams, 3,200-member association, 8,000 subscribers, 18-session track), consistent with the KB values.

## Sentence and register
- Average sentence length in the letters is 26 to 39 words (aphsa 38.7, nami 32.0, governors-pha 26.4, ndi 26.8). Long compound sentences with subordinate clauses are normal, followed by a short sentence for emphasis.
- First person singular, plain declaratives, no exclamation marks, no slang. Humble, learning-first tone about gaps; confident about results.
- Numbers in letters are often written as words in prose ("twenty to thirty", "six or more", "a half dozen platforms"), with numerals for percentages and counts in lists (35 percent, 8,000 subscribers).
- Em dashes are not used in any of the ten letters and not in writing sample 1; sample 2 (a published blog) uses four. Keep em dashes out of letters (also the user's standing preference for narrative prose).

## Longer-form writing (blogs, briefs)
- Titles use metaphor ("Tending the Soil: Why Recovery-Ready Families Are the Missing Infrastructure of Addiction Treatment", writing sample 1). Openings use a vivid image or reversal ("The waiting room has always been the wrong metaphor", sample 1; "The clamor of the casino floor has given way to something quieter", sample 2).
- Sections have short headings, a bolded key-facts list where useful (sample 2), and evidence is linked inline with citation links (both samples).
- A section of numbered, imperative "principles" ends the argument (sample 1), followed by a short closing image.

## Things to avoid
- Do not state a number, date, title or credential that is not in the KB. Use the KB's canonical value and ask if an open conflict exists.
- Do not describe any role as full-time or part-time.
- Do not add publication-status details for Recovery Ready.
- NCMW title is always "Project Coordinator".
- Do not copy a cover-letter paragraph verbatim between applications; the same arc is reused but each opening names the specific organization's work.

## Variations found (not conflicts)
- Letters differ on small scope wording for the same facts ("two to three concurrent labs" in aphsa, "three" in governors-pha and ndi; "four separate housing subsidy systems" in ndi, "five" elsewhere). Per repo rules these are scope variants to be bracketed in the KB (already: A-sanctuary-centers-housing-coordinator-07 "four [five]"). Use the KB value in new drafts.
