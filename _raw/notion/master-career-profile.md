## 3.3 Ibogaine Research & Environmental Scan
**Primary sources:** Project_Brief_Ibogaine_Research.md; Project_Brief_Ibogaine_Scan.md **Cross-referenced with:** Resume bullet 22
### Overview (from brief)
Comprehensive research effort into ibogaine, a psychoactive indole alkaloid derived from the West African shrub *Tabernanthe iboga*, and its therapeutic potential in treating substance use disorders, particularly opioid use disorder. Work compiled an environmental scan of the current U.S. and international research landscape alongside a curated literature review of peer-reviewed clinical studies. Ibogaine has a long history of ceremonial use in West Central Africa (Babongo and Mitsogo peoples); it works through a novel mechanism targeting multiple receptor systems simultaneously; it is a **Schedule I substance in the United States** but legally available or unscheduled in several other countries, providing unique conditions for observational clinical research.
### Literature review scope
**12 peer-reviewed publications and one active clinical trial record (NCT05029401)** reviewed, spanning foundational early research through contemporary systematic reviews. Key sources with findings:
- **Noller et al. (2018)** — 12-month observational study (**n=14**) measuring addiction severity via ASI-Lite following ibogaine treatment in New Zealand, where ibogaine is legal. Documented sustained reductions in opioid dependence and secondary improvements in depression.
- **Brown & Alper (2018)** — Observational study (**n=30**) tracking detoxification and drug use outcomes at 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months post-treatment. At one month, **50%** of subjects reported no opioid use in the prior 30 days, compared to **18%** with buprenorphine.
- **Davis et al. (2017)** — Survey of **88 patients** treated in Mexico (2012–2015). **80%** reported ibogaine eliminated or drastically reduced withdrawal symptoms; **30%** reported never using opioids again after treatment.
- **Mash et al. (2018)** — Open-label case series (**n=191**) examining ibogaine for opioid and cocaine detoxification under medical monitoring. No significant adverse events recorded within study parameters.
- **Köck et al. (2022)** — Systematic literature review (PRISMA) covering **24 studies** and **705 individuals**. Confirmed efficacy in reducing withdrawal and craving, with noted cardiac safety concerns.
- **Mosca et al. (2023)** — Systematic review of **31 articles** confirming anti-addictive effects while flagging cardiotoxicity and mortality as primary safety concerns.
- **Luz & Mash (2021)** — Editorial review of toxicity and therapeutic potential, emphasizing QT prolongation, CYP2D6 metabolism, and cardiac monitoring protocols.
- **Davis et al. (2023)** — Open-label study of combined ibogaine and 5-MeO-DMT therapy for trauma-exposed male Special Operations Forces Veterans, conducted in Mexico. Significant improvements in PTSD, depression, and anxiety measures.
- **Alper et al. (1998)** — Foundational *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* paper reviewing early safety data, pharmacokinetics, preliminary clinical observations.
- **Lao et al. (UC Davis)** — Comparative nursing/PA student review of ibogaine vs. buprenorphine efficacy; concluded ibogaine shows promise but lacks sufficient controlled trial data for formal practice recommendations.

**Safety concerns.** Cardiotoxicity, specifically QT interval prolongation and associated risk of fatal arrhythmia, is the central safety concern across all reviewed literature. Several fatalities reported, predominantly outside medically supervised settings. Cardiac screening and monitoring consistently emphasized as prerequisites.
**Research gaps.** Field lacks large-scale randomized controlled trials; most existing data comes from observational studies, retrospective surveys, and open-label designs, limiting efficacy claims and making formal practice recommendations premature.
**Pharmaceutical development trajectory.** Most active area involves structural analogues designed to retain anti-addictive properties while eliminating cardiotoxicity and hallucinogenic effects. **GM-1020** (Gilgamesh) and **DLX-007** (Delix) are the leading candidates as of the scan date.

## 3.5 Behavioral Healthtech Consulting Proposal
**Primary source:** Project_Draft_Healthtech_Consulting_Proposal.md **Cross-referenced with:** Resume bullet 31
### Executive summary (from proposal)
Behavioral health leaders are no longer making technology decisions once every decade; they are being asked to make them continuously, including what to buy, how to fund it, how to integrate it into care delivery, and how to ensure each decision supports the organization's financial health. Organizations need new internal skillsets across clinical, operational, and executive teams. Historically excluded from federal EHR investments, behavioral health providers now face a surge of technology options, vendors, and funding pathways with limited technology literacy and vendor-neutral guidance. **Member requests for support are currently being routed to external consultants such as RSM and Metta Health**, highlighting both the urgency and scale of demand. Establishing a National Council-led Healthtech Consulting Pilot would allow the Council to test delivering trusted, field-informed advisory services.
### Option 1: Full-Scale Consulting Arm
**Five service domains and deliverables:**
1. **Readiness Assessment & Technology Review** — Services: organizational assessments, technology stack evaluations, data infrastructure analysis. Deliverables: Maturity Scorecard, Tech Stack Report, Gap Analysis.
2. **Strategic Planning & Roadmapping** — Services: facilitated strategy sessions, prioritization of use cases, ROI modeling. Deliverables: Strategic Roadmap, Use Case Framework, Investment Summary.
3. **Technical Assistance & Implementation Support** — Services: vendor selection support, pilot program design, change management, go-live assistance. Deliverables: Vendor Shortlist, Pilot Plan, Implementation Toolkit, Readiness Review.
4. **Training & Workforce Enablement** — Services: role-specific training, responsible AI education, superuser development. Deliverables: Training Modules, AI Guidelines, Train-the-Trainer Kits, Learning Plans.
5. **Managed Services: Analytics, Reporting & Execution** — Services: ongoing analytics support, compliance reporting, strategic insight generation. Deliverables: Dashboards, Reporting Templates, Strategic Memos.
**Tiered pricing (project detail dependent):**
- Readiness Snapshots: **\$25,000–\$40,000**
- Roadmap Sprints: **\$50,000–\$75,000**
- End-to-End Pilots: **\$125,000–\$200,000**
- Advisory Retainers: **\$30,000–\$60,000 per quarter**
- Cohort Programs: **\$10,000–\$20,000 per organization**
**Staffing and financials:**
- Approximately **8.5 FTEs** including a director, project managers, technical consultants, trainers, analysts, and administrative support
- **Year 1: \$1.28 million revenue, \$1.65 million overhead, resulting in a \$370,000 loss**
- **Year 2: \$2.875 million revenue, resulting in a \$1.2 million profit**
**Advantages:** national reach and brand credibility; comprehensive service offerings; strong positioning in the behavioral health sector.
### Option 2: Bootstrapped Consulting Arm
**Modified service domains and deliverables:**
1. **Readiness Assessment** — basic interviews and technology inventory tools → Basic Scorecard, Summary Report
2. **Strategic Planning** — remote facilitation using standardized templates → Strategic Roadmap, Use Case Brief, Resource Worksheet
3. **Technical Assistance (Limited)** — vendor research and templated pilot support → Vendor Table, Pilot Template
4. **Training (Limited)** — one-time workshops and introductory content → Presentation Slides, AI Overview, Train-the-Trainer Materials
5. **Managed Services** — deferred for later implementation phases
**Tiered pricing (project detail and scope dependent):**
- Readiness Snapshot: **\$20,000–\$30,000**
- Roadmap Sprint: **\$40,000–\$60,000**
- Retainers: **\$5,000–\$10,000 per month**
- Trainings: **\$10,000–\$15,000**
- Pilot Support: **\$25,000–\$40,000**
**Staffing and financials:**
- Approximately **2 FTEs** plus contractor support
- **Year 1: \$500,000 revenue, \$295,000 overhead, resulting in \~40% profit**
- **Year 2: \$850,000–\$1.1 million revenue** with gradual hiring
**Advantages:** faster launch and lower financial risk; scalable framework for future growth; ability to test and refine the consulting model.
### Conclusion (from proposal)
Both options offer a strategic pathway for the National Council for Mental Wellbeing to lead the behavioral health sector in ethical and effective technology adoption. The full-scale option delivers deep capacity and nationwide impact; the bootstrapped model enables a nimble, low-risk market entry. Each supports the Council's mission and enhances its role as a trusted partner in the digital transformation of behavioral health services.

- Peer support: four domains (emotional, informational, instrumental, affiliational); measurable improvements in family member self-efficacy, personal wellbeing, and social connectedness
- Naloxone: available over the counter in **all 50 states**; CDC recommends for anyone in proximity to a person at elevated overdose risk; **only one naloxone prescription is dispensed for every seventy high-dose opioid prescriptions**
- Diversity gap evidence: doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2017-019433
